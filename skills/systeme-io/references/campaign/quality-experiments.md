# Quality review and measured improvement

## Before publication

Inspect the complete rendered page, including its surrounding template. Check desktop and a narrow mobile view. Verify content order, line lengths, spacing, columns, tables, image cropping, video dimensions and absence of duplicate covers. Read the page as someone who has never heard of the product.

Check semantic headings, readable contrast, form labels, keyboard focus, validation/error states and reduced motion. Test every CTA destination and required referral parameter when provided. Do not remove essential disclosures or billing information to make a layout look cleaner.

Confirm the public page's title/description and selected social image. Check indexability only when relevant to the task; a correct setup cannot guarantee indexing or rankings. Optimize image size and unnecessary scripts without breaking the platform's native behavior.

Test the form-to-follow-up path with an authorized address. Compare promised resource, confirmation page and delivery message. Review campaign timing and duplicate automations. Separate a configured trigger from an observed successful execution.

When a check fails, fix the smallest cause and rerun the affected check. Avoid broad repeated audits once meaningful checks pass. Save useful proof in the user's selected campaign/output folder, not a cluttered root.

## After launch

Define the conversion before analyzing results: action count / eligible visits, with a stated period and audience. Establish a baseline and inspect the traffic source/device mix. No analytics means no measured conversion claim.

Use a hypothesis tied to an observed problem: "Visitors cannot tell what the download contains; a concrete preview may increase qualified downloads." Change one meaningful variable when feasible, such as headline, offer explanation or CTA location. Choose the primary outcome and guardrails before testing, and do not stop early merely because one version briefly leads.

Use the platform's supported test method. Low-traffic sites may be better served by qualitative review and fixing obvious friction before statistical claims. Preserve a rollback/version record. Report the period, sample, outcome and limitations; do not call a professional redesign "high-converting" without actual results.
